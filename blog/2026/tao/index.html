This is normal bad work

[This guest post is an expanded version of a post on my own blog]

When you prove a theorem, you are imparted a responsibility to the field. At minimum you need to explain what you did, including which steps required new ideas and which steps are already well-known. More substantively, you are expected to nurture the literature. If an important idea was poorly explained in its first incarnation, then you have a duty to explain it better in your work. The responsibility is yours because you have the opportunity to publish a paper on the subject.

These demands are broadly accepted and are not controversial. Any time you exceed the demands, the resulting paper is highly appreciated and valued. Any time you fail the demands, you will get a "revise and resubmit" for a journal submission, or a straight rejection for a conference. Until you fix your exposition, your ideas will not enter the canon. If you post unpolished manuscripts to the arXiv, you are embarrassing yourself.

Compare this to OpenAI's behavior. They do not care about their "preprints". They don't bother putting in appropriate attribution of ideas, they salami-slice one result over multiple pdfs, their work contains retraction-worthy errors, and they don't even bother with consistent presentation or formatting.[1] Their pdfs actively set back the state of the literature. This is poor quality work and these pdfs are all easy rejects.

We all know that OpenAI is not doing this 'for the good of science'. These pdfs are exhaust fumes from their internal benchmarking effort. We do not need them to release this unprocessed exhaust into the atmosphere. OpenAI's new internal model produced these texts with the press of a button. But similar results would have been found soon by experts anyway, with or without LLM prompting. In the October 6th drop, OpenAI scooped several of their customers. Even without alleging theft, it is a bad look for a supplier of scientific software to actively scoop their customers.

If an expert prompts a result, they take up the mantle of responsibility I describe above. OpenAI not only refuses to take their responsibility, but they prevent their own customers from doing so. And they have the audacity to say they do it for the good of science.

OpenAI's droppings on github dot com are not a watershed moment for mathematics. It is normal progress: normal for the current state of technology. Or at least normal for the available technology in 2 months from now. The only thing that is exceptional is OpenAI's lack of shame for releasing poor quality work.
